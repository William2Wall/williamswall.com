EXECUTIVE INSIGHT 01

Organizations Produce Exactly the Results They Were Designed to Produce

If you've led people for any length of time, you've probably experienced it.

  • A strategic initiative loses momentum.
  • A high-performing employee leaves out of frustration.
  • Meetings multiply while decisions slow.

Another reorganization promises to solve the problem, yet somehow the same challenges return six months later.

Most organizations respond the same way. They hire new leaders, implement new technology, reorganize departments, launch another transformation initiative, or ask people to work harder. Those efforts can certainly help, but the improvements rarely last.

The reason is surprisingly simple.

Most organizational problems are not people problems. They are organizational design problems.

Every organization has an operating model, whether intentionally designed or allowed to evolve over time. That operating model determines how decisions are made, how authority is exercised, how work flows across teams, and how accountability is reinforced. It quietly shapes culture, execution, trust, innovation, and ultimately, organizational performance.

The Executive Operating Model begins with a different question.

Instead of asking, "Why did this happen?"

Ask, "Did we intentionally design the system that produced this outcome, or did we simply allow it to emerge?"

That single question shifts the conversation away from symptoms and toward the operating model itself.

Changing people can improve performance.

Changing the operating model can improve the organization.

Understanding the difference is where leadership becomes transformational.

EXECUTIVE INSIGHT 02

Why Good People Struggle in Broken Systems

Organizations invest enormous time and resources recruiting exceptional people. They search for experience, leadership potential, technical expertise, and cultural fit because they believe the right people will naturally produce better outcomes.

Then something unexpected happens:

  • An exceptional employee suddenly begins underperforming.
  • A respected manager burns out.
  • A talented project leader becomes frustrated.
  • A new hire who looked outstanding during the interview struggles to gain traction.

Leaders naturally look for problems in people because people are visible. We coach them, train them, evaluate them, and, when necessary, replace them. More often, capable people are asked to succeed within systems that make repeatable success unnecessarily difficult.

Systems are much harder to see. They quietly influence how work gets done long before anyone notices the symptoms they create. Poorly designed operating models create friction that even exceptional people struggle to overcome.

Conflicting priorities, unclear decision authority, competing incentives, excessive approvals, and inconsistent accountability don't frustrate people because they lack talent. They frustrate people because they're attempting to succeed within a system that wasn't designed to support success.

The question isn't whether your people are capable. The question is whether your operating model allows them to be.

People perform at their best when the system around them is designed to help them succeed. They should not have to overcome the system in order to succeed.

EXECUTIVE INSIGHT 03

Execution Is Designed, Not Managed

Organizations don't struggle because people are unwilling to execute. They struggle because execution is often treated as an activity instead of an organizational capability.

Leaders ask teams to move faster despite slower decision-making. They demand accountability while authority remains unclear. They expect collaboration while competing priorities pull departments in different directions.

The result is predictable. Organizations become dependent on heroic effort instead of well-designed systems.

When organizations consistently rely on heroics, long hours, and constant executive intervention just to achieve expected results, execution isn't the problem. They're symptoms of an operating model that was never designed to support repeatable success.

Exceptional organizations think differently.

  • They don't manage execution. They design for it.
  • They define ownership and establish clear decision rights.
  • They reduce unnecessary friction and align priorities.
  • They create operating rhythms that help people make better decisions and execute with confidence.

Execution isn't something leaders demand. It's something they intentionally enable through the organizations they design.
